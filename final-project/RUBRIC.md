# Final Project Rubric

Total: **30%** of the course grade. Deliverable weights: notebook 15%, report 10%, presentation 5%.
The five criteria below cover all three deliverables. Score each criterion with the level that best fits, then multiply by its weight.

| Level | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Excellent | 100% |
| Good | 80% |
| Developing | 60% |
| Beginning | 30% |
| Missing | 0% |

## Criteria

### 1. Data cleaning and preparation — 6%
| Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Types, missing values, duplicates and outliers are handled with a stated reason for each choice; useful features are engineered and correctly encoded; no data leakage. | Most issues handled sensibly; one or two choices not justified. | Some cleaning, but important issues (e.g. missing values or wrong types) are left or handled inappropriately. | Little or no cleaning; analysis runs on raw data. |

### 2. Exploration and visualization — 6%
| Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chart types match the data and question, with a sentence explaining each choice; charts are labelled and titles state findings. | Appropriate charts, mostly labelled; explanations are brief. | Several unsuitable or unlabelled charts; little interpretation. | Few or misleading charts; no interpretation. |

### 3. Statistical analysis — 6%
| Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive statistics summarise the data; at least one correctly chosen hypothesis test or regression with H₀/H₁, assumptions checked, and the result explained in plain language with an effect size. | Correct test or regression with minor gaps in assumptions or interpretation. | A test is run but poorly chosen or misinterpreted (e.g. “p > 0.05 proves H₀”). | No inferential analysis, or results are wrong. |

### 4. Machine learning model — 7%
| Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suitable model for the problem; train/test split (and scaling inside a pipeline where needed); compared with a baseline; appropriate metrics (accuracy + confusion matrix, or RMSE/R², or elbow/cluster interpretation); honest discussion of limits. | Sound model and evaluation with one gap (e.g. no baseline). | Model trained but evaluated on training data or with an unsuitable metric. | Model missing or not working. |

### 5. Communication (report and presentation) — 5%
| Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report (2–3 pages) and talk (5–8 min) tell a clear story for a non-technical audience: question → method → findings → recommendation → limitations. Notebook is tidy and runs top to bottom. | Clear overall with some jargon or a weak conclusion. | Hard to follow; findings not linked to the question. | Missing or unreadable deliverables. |

## Feedback

Instructors return written feedback on each criterion. In facilitated settings, learners may revise and resubmit one deliverable within one week.
