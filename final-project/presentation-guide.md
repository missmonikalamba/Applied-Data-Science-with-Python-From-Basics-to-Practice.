# Presentation guide (5–8 minutes)

Your audience is **non-technical**: a manager, a community group, a policy maker. They want to know what you found and what to do about it, not how pandas works.

You may present live with slides, or record a walkthrough (screen recording with voice). Upload slides as PDF, or put the recording link in your repository README.

## Suggested structure (6–8 slides)

| # | Slide | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | 15 s | Project title, your name, the question in one sentence |
| 2 | Why it matters | 45 s | Who cares about this question and why |
| 3 | The data | 45 s | Source, what one row is, size, one data-quality issue you fixed |
| 4 | Key finding 1 | 1 min | One chart, title states the finding |
| 5 | Key finding 2 (statistics) | 1 min | Test or regression result in plain words, with effect size |
| 6 | The model | 1 min | What it predicts or groups, how well vs a baseline, in everyday units |
| 7 | Recommendation | 45 s | What the audience should do |
| 8 | Limitations | 30 s | What could make this wrong; next steps |

## Tips
- One message per slide; put the message in the slide title.
- Replace jargon: say “predictions are typically off by about $50,000” rather than “RMSE = 50,000”.
- Rehearse once with a timer.
- You may reuse the course slide template (any module deck in this repository) for a consistent look.
